W12 - Compare communication methods using evidence
Command word: Compare
Opening: Both methods transmitted successfully in all 10 trials without an obstruction.

Gap 1: Compare the obstructed results.
CORRECT A | comparison-a
With the screen, infrared succeeded in 2/10 trials and Bluetooth in 9/10 trials.
Feedback: Yes: this comparison uses both successful-transmission values.
DISTRACTOR B | comparison-b
With the screen, infrared succeeded in 9/10 trials and Bluetooth in 2/10 trials.
Feedback: Those two results have been swapped.
DISTRACTOR C | comparison-c
With the screen, both methods still succeeded in all 10 of the trials.
Feedback: 10/10 applies to the unobstructed test, not this row.
DISTRACTOR D | comparison-d
With the screen, infrared succeeded in 8/10 trials and Bluetooth in 1/10 trials.
Feedback: Those are the numbers of failed trials. The table gives successes.

Gap 2: Explain the infrared result.
DISTRACTOR A | infrared-a
Infrared needs no clear path, so the screen should not affect reception.
Feedback: The obstructed results show why a clear path matters here.
CORRECT B | infrared-b
Infrared needs a clear path, and the screen blocked much of its signal.
Feedback: Yes: a blocked path explains the large reduction in successful reception.
DISTRACTOR C | infrared-c
Infrared cannot travel 4 m, even when there is no screen in the path.
Feedback: The 10/10 unobstructed result shows that 4 m was within range.
DISTRACTOR D | infrared-d
Infrared has a lower frequency after the screen, so the receiver stops working.
Feedback: Frequency does not change simply because light enters a stationary material.

Gap 3: Explain the Bluetooth result.
DISTRACTOR A | bluetooth-a
Bluetooth uses infrared waves that are unaffected by this screen.
Feedback: Bluetooth uses radio waves, not infrared.
DISTRACTOR B | bluetooth-b
Bluetooth travels faster than infrared, reaching the receiver before an obstruction matters.
Feedback: Both are electromagnetic waves; a higher speed does not explain these results.
DISTRACTOR C | bluetooth-c
Bluetooth gains energy in the plastic screen, increasing the strength of the signal.
Feedback: The screen does not add energy to strengthen the signal.
CORRECT D | bluetooth-d
Bluetooth uses radio waves that passed through this screen well enough for reception.
Feedback: Yes: the radio signal remained usable through this particular material.

Gap 4: Suggest a method and justify it for this room.
DISTRACTOR A | recommendation-a
Use infrared because both methods were equally successful when the path was clear.
Feedback: This room has frequent obstructions, so the obstructed results matter too.
DISTRACTOR B | recommendation-b
Use Bluetooth because its signal will pass through every material in the room.
Feedback: The test supports a choice for this obstruction; it does not prove passage through every material.
CORRECT C | recommendation-c
Use Bluetooth because interruptions are likely and it worked better with an obstruction.
Feedback: Yes: this links the test evidence to people regularly crossing the path.
DISTRACTOR D | recommendation-d
Use infrared because fewer successful transmissions mean less unwanted interference.
Feedback: Fewer successful transmissions mean poorer reliability for the intended task.

W27 - Explain transmission in an optical fibre
Command word: Explain
Opening: The ray reaches the core-cladding boundary from inside the core.

Gap 1: Compare the refractive indices.
DISTRACTOR A | indices-a
The core has a lower refractive index than the cladding.
Feedback: 1.50 is greater than 1.40; the direction is reversed.
DISTRACTOR B | indices-b
The core and cladding have equal refractive indices.
Feedback: The supplied indices are different.
CORRECT C | indices-c
The core has a greater refractive index than the cladding.
Feedback: Yes: light approaches the boundary from the higher-index material.
DISTRACTOR D | indices-d
Light travels faster in the core than in the cladding.
Feedback: A greater refractive index means a lower light speed.

Gap 2: Relate the incidence angle to the critical angle.
CORRECT A | angle-a
The incidence angle is greater than the critical angle at this boundary.
Feedback: Yes: 75° exceeds the supplied critical angle of 69°.
DISTRACTOR B | angle-b
The incidence angle must equal the critical angle at this boundary.
Feedback: Equality would give a refracted ray along the boundary.
DISTRACTOR C | angle-c
The incidence angle is less than the critical angle at this boundary.
Feedback: 75° is greater than 69°, not less.
DISTRACTOR D | angle-d
Any non-zero incidence angle is sufficient at this boundary.
Feedback: The angle must exceed the critical angle, not merely exceed zero.

Gap 3: Explain how the light remains in the core.
DISTRACTOR A | process-a
Light refracts into the cladding and then reflects from the outer surface.
Feedback: The required reflection is at the core-cladding boundary.
DISTRACTOR B | process-b
Light is absorbed by the core and re-emitted at each boundary.
Feedback: Absorption and re-emission are not the TIR mechanism.
CORRECT C | process-c
Light is totally internally reflected back into the core at successive boundaries.
Feedback: Yes: repeated TIR keeps the ray in the core.
DISTRACTOR D | process-d
Light is reflected because it approaches the boundary from the lower-index material.
Feedback: The light approaches from the higher-index core.
